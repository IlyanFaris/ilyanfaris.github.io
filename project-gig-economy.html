AI and algorithmic management in Indonesia’s gig economy

Institute of International Studies (IIS) UGM · 2026 · Research Assistant

Summary

This project studied how Indonesian gig workers understand and respond to algorithmic management. It compared online motorcycle taxi drivers, freelance photographers and unionised creative workers through deliberative workshops and surveys run before, during and after the workshops.

The main finding was the opposite of what we expected. Workers’ capacity for technological citizenship followed their access to collective organisation, not how much they were exposed to algorithms. Union-affiliated creative workers changed most; taxi drivers, despite the deepest platform experience, did not change at all. We call this the deliberative infrastructure gap.

What I did

I facilitated focus group discussions with gig worker communities, including ride-hailing drivers and photographers, with around 60 participants in total. I co-authored the resulting paper and presented the findings to graduate students and academic audiences.
