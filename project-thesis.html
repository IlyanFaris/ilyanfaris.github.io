AI colonialism in creative platform labour

Undergraduate thesis, Universitas Gadjah Mada · 2026 · Supervised by Dr. Suci Lestari Yuana

Summary

Fotoyu is an Indonesian AI-based photography marketplace that has come to dominate event photography. It uses facial recognition to match photos with clients and automates payment and distribution. Most platform labour research looks at ride-hailing and delivery, and most AI colonialism research looks at extraction from North to South. This thesis asks what happens where the two meet: creative platform work, and colonial dynamics inside the Global South.

Approach

Working within critical political economy and AI colonialism scholarship, I ran focus group discussions with twenty Indonesian freelance photographers and combined them with secondary data and an analysis of the platform itself.

Findings

The thesis identifies three mechanisms through which Fotoyu reproduces colonial relations: dependency, which locks photographers into the platform’s discovery and pricing systems; heteromation, the unpaid cognitive and creative work of correcting the platform’s AI failures; and displaced discipline, where enforcement is pushed onto peers and built into the platform’s design. Photographers do resist, but individually, in ways the platform’s structure largely forecloses.

The argument is that colonial extraction is defined by operational logic rather than geography: a domestic platform can act as a colonial actor within a single country.
